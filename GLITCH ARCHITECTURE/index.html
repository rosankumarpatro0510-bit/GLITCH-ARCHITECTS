Nowcast window 0–6 hours · hyper-local · global coverage

Severe weather arrivesfaster than a forecast cycle.

A thunderstorm cell can go from clear sky to a hundred millimetres of rain in under ninety minutes. Glitch Architecture watches that window — scoring thunderstorm, cloudburst and flash-flood risk cell by cell, showing which people and infrastructure sit underneath it, and explaining in plain language why the number moved.

Why the 0–6 hour window is the hard one

Daily forecasts handle tomorrow well. What they handle badly is the convective cell that forms over one catchment this afternoon, dumps its rain in forty minutes, and floods a specific set of streets. That is where the deaths happen, and where the warning has to be local, fast, and explainable enough that someone will act on it.

40 min

From growth to ground

A cumulonimbus can go from first cooling cloud tops to peak rainfall in well under an hour. Anything slower than a nowcast misses it.

2 km

The scale that matters

Cloudbursts and urban flash floods concentrate at a scale far below a district bulletin. One ward floods while the next stays dry.

Uneven

Data is not evenly good

Radar coverage, rain gauges and OpenStreetMap infrastructure are all patchy — especially rurally. A system that hides that is lying to its user.

Why?

Numbers alone don't move people

A control-room officer acts on a score they can interrogate. Every prediction here opens up into the indicators that produced it.

What this build does not do

A disaster tool that overstates itself is worse than no tool. So this is stated up front, and repeated inside every module it applies to.

Read this before you trust a number on screen

This is a demonstration prototype built for Smart India Hackathon. The risk engine is a deterministic, physically-motivated model — not a system trained on observations, and not connected to a live feed.

  • No live satellite, radar, rain-gauge or NWP data is ingested. The adapter interfaces are specified and documented; the endpoints are not connected.
  • No accuracy, skill score or lead-time figure is claimed anywhere, because no verification run has been performed.
  • Alerts are generated from demonstration thresholds and are labelled as such. None of them is a government warning.
  • Routes are compared by modelled exposure. A lower-exposure route is described as comparatively lower risk — never as safe.
  • Exposure counts depend on the geospatial data available, which is uneven. Absence from a list is not absence on the ground.
  • Citizen reports are public observations and are never promoted into the model as measurements.

For official severe-weather warnings in India, follow the India Meteorological Department, the National Disaster Management Authority and your State Disaster Management Authority. Emergency number 112. NDMA helpline 1078.
